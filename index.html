Responsible AI: Understanding and Addressing Bias

Artificial intelligence can support decisions, but it can also repeat unfair patterns found in data. Explore three real-world situations and test your understanding of responsible AI.

Why does AI bias matter?

AI systems learn patterns from the data and instructions used to build them. When that information is incomplete, unbalanced, or reflects past unfairness, the system may produce results that disadvantage certain people. Human review and ongoing testing are important.

Example 1 · Healthcare

AI in healthcare

Imagine a healthcare system that uses an AI tool to help identify patients who may need extra medical attention. If the data used to train the tool mostly represents one patient group, the tool may be less accurate for people from groups that were not well represented.

Possible impact

Some patients may not be identified as needing care when they do. This could contribute to unequal treatment or delayed support.

Ways to reduce bias

  • Use training data that represents different patient populations.
  • Test accuracy across relevant groups, not only overall accuracy.
  • Keep qualified healthcare professionals involved in decisions.
Think about it: Why is it important to test an AI healthcare tool with data from different patient groups?

Testing across different groups can reveal whether the tool works less accurately for some patients. Finding these gaps gives developers and healthcare teams a chance to improve the system before relying on it.
